🎯 Master Problem Patterns, Not Individual Solutions

Coding platforms like LeetCode and AlgoMonster have thousands of problems. Memorizing code line-by-line fails in interviews. AlgoRecall uses the scientific Free Spaced Repetition Scheduler (FSRS) to model your personal memory retention decay curve. It calculates exactly when you should re-solve patterns to dramatically reduce study time (saving 20–30%+ review time) while maximizing long-term retention.

🧠 FSRS: Your Smart Memory Coach

Think of FSRS (Free Spaced Repetition Scheduler) as your personal study superpower! Instead of re-solving hundreds of LeetCode or AlgoMonster problems over and over, FSRS learns how fast your brain forgets code patterns. It schedules your reviews right at the moment you're about to forget them—so you remember more with way less effort!

⚡ Study 30% Less, Remember 90% More!

By skipping problems you already know well and focusing strictly on tricky ones, FSRS cuts your daily review time by 20% to 30%+ compared to old-school flashcard apps. You'll master more interview patterns in significantly fewer total practice sessions!

🌱 The 4 Stages of Your Flashcards

Every coding problem you add goes on a journey through 4 simple stages:

Stage 0: New

🆕 Fresh Card

A problem you just added. You haven't practiced or tested yourself on it yet.

Stage 1: Learning

📖 Practice Mode

You're building initial memory. The card shows up in short practice rounds (like 1 min or 10 mins) until you've got it down.

Stage 2: Review

🏆 Mastered!

It's locked in! The card is now in long-term memory. FSRS will space out reviews (4 days, 12 days, 1 month...) to keep it fresh.

Stage 3: Relearning

🔄 Quick Refresher

Forgot a mastered problem? No stress! FSRS gives you a quick refresher round until it's safely back in long-term memory.

🎯 How to Grade Yourself (Button Guide)

🔴 Again

"I blanked out / Got stuck"
Couldn't recall the main pattern, or forgot a key step of the algorithm. (Triggers quick re-test).

🟡 Hard

"I got it, but it was a struggle!"
You solved it correctly, but required heavy effort, hesitation, or made minor syntax slip-ups.

🟢 Good (Default Choice!)

"Smooth recall!"
You remembered the approach and solved it in standard time. Make this your main button ~70-80% of the time!

🔵 Easy

"Piece of cake!"
Felt completely obvious or trivial. Pushes the next review date far into the future.

🧩 What If I Only Remember SOME of the Problem?

It happens all the time! Here is a simple rule of thumb to decide what to press:

The Interview Reality Check: Missing Core Logic? → Press 🔴 Again

Ask yourself: "If an interviewer gave me this problem right now, would my missing piece make me get stuck?"
If YES (for example: you remembered Two Pointers but forgot how to handle duplicate triplets in 3Sum, or forgot the DP base case), press 🔴 Again. It's totally okay to fail a card—that's how your brain builds bulletproof recall!

Main Algorithm Solid, Just Paused on Syntax? → Press 🟡 Hard

If your high-level approach and logic were 100% correct, but you just briefly paused on language syntax (like queue or map function names), press 🟡 Hard.

💡 Student Tip: The "Break-It-Down" Rule

If a problem note feels huge and you always forget half of it, break it into 2 smaller cards! For example: Card #1 = High-Level Pattern & Time Complexity, Card #2 = Tricky Edge Cases & Base Cases.

🧠 Understanding FSRS Parameters (w0 – w16)

FSRS uses 17 coefficients to compute memory stability updates upon grading:

  • Target Retention Slider (0.75 - 0.95): Sets desired recall probability. Higher retention (95%) yields shorter intervals; lower retention (80%) spreads reviews further apart.
  • Initial Stability (w0 – w3): Base interval days for Again, Hard, Good, Easy on first review.
  • Difficulty & Stability Boosts (w4 – w10): Rate of stability increase for correct answers.
  • Lapses & Easy Bonuses (w11 – w16): Recovery deceleration upon failure and easy bonuses.

🏷️ Tag-Specific Profiles

Assign custom 17-coefficient weight sets to specific tags (e.g. Dynamic Programming or Graphs) to handle harder topics with customized memory parameters.
