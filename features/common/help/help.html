🌱 The 4 Stages of Your Flashcards

Every coding problem you add goes on a journey through 4 simple stages: New (0), Learning (1), Review (2), and Relearning (3).

Stage 0: New

🆕 Fresh Problem

A problem you just added. You haven't practiced or tested yourself on it yet. Created when logging problem notes. Awaits initial rating in floating bar.

  • Again / Hard: → Moves to Learning
  • Good / Easy: → Graduates to Review
  • Reps / Lapses: 0 reps, 0 lapses
Stage 1: Learning

📖 Practice Mode

You're building initial memory. The card shows up in short practice rounds (like 1 min or 10 mins) until you've got it down.

  • Again: Resets step timer back to Step 1
  • Hard: Stays in short practice
  • Good / Easy: → Promotes to Review
Stage 2: Review

🏆 Mastered

It's locked in! The card is now in long-term memory. The AlgoRecall Engine will space out reviews (4 days, 12 days, 1 month...) to keep it fresh.

  • Hard / Good / Easy: Stays in Review (interval grows)
  • Again (Lapse): → Demoted to Relearning
  • Milestone: Stability > 7d = Graduated
Stage 3: Relearning

🔄 Refresher

Forgot a mastered problem? No stress! The AlgoRecall Engine gives you a quick refresher round until it's safely back in long-term memory.

  • Again: Resets refresher timer
  • Hard: Continues refresher practice
  • Good / Easy: → Returns to Review

🧩 What If I Only Remember SOME of the Problem?

It happens all the time! Here is a simple rule of thumb to decide what to press:

The Interview Reality Check: Missing Core Logic? → Press 🔴 Again

Ask yourself: "If an interviewer gave me this problem right now, would my missing piece make me get stuck?"
If YES (for example: you remembered Two Pointers but forgot how to handle duplicate triplets in 3Sum, or forgot the DP base case), press 🔴 Again. It's totally okay to fail a card—that's how your brain builds bulletproof recall!

Main Algorithm Solid, Just Paused on Syntax? → Press 🟡 Hard

If your high-level approach and logic were 100% correct, but you just briefly paused on language syntax (like queue or map function names), press 🟡 Hard.

💡 Student Tip: The "Break-It-Down" Rule

If a problem note feels huge and you always forget half of it, break it into 2 smaller cards! For example: Card #1 = High-Level Pattern & Time Complexity, Card #2 = Tricky Edge Cases & Base Cases.

⏰ What Happens When a Review Is Overdue?

The AlgoRecall Engine tracks elapsed days (actual days since last review) separately from scheduled days.

  • Reviewed On Time: The AlgoRecall Engine predicts retrievability R ≈ 88% and expands interval normally.
  • Overdue + Remembered (Good/Easy): If you review 5 days late and still recall it, the AlgoRecall Engine realizes your memory is stronger than expected! It awards a higher stability boost than normal.
  • Overdue + Forgotten (Again): Stability drops sharply and the card enters Relearning. Do not fear lapses; they protect you from false confidence.
  • Cards Never Expire: Overdue cards simply wait in queue sorted by due date; progress is never wiped out automatically.

🔬 Core AlgoRecall Engine Concepts in Plain English

You don't need a math degree to understand the AlgoRecall Engine. Here is what the core parameters mean in everyday terms:

1. Stability (S) — "Memory Strength"

Stability is the number of days it takes for your recall probability to fall from 100% down to your target retention (88%). If a card has S = 30, it means you can leave it untouched for an entire month and still have an 88% chance of remembering it!

2. Difficulty (D) — "Inherent Complexity"

Ranked from 1 (trivial) to 10 (exceptionally tough). Harder cards gain stability more slowly with each successful review, meaning the AlgoRecall Engine spaces them out more conservatively so they don't slip away.

3. Retrievability (R) — "Recall Probability Right Now"

A dynamic score between 0% and 100% indicating how likely you are to solve the problem today. Immediately after reviewing, R = 100%. Over time, it follows the forgetting curve.

4. Rapid clicking & 1-Minute Misclick Correction

If you accidentally click Good instead of Again, simply press Again within 60 seconds! The scheduler automatically restores the card's previous state and recalculates cleanly without skewing your history.

🛡️ Your Responsibilities: How to Use the Extension Correctly

The AlgoRecall Engine is a powerful mathematical model, but its predictions rely 100% on the accuracy of your feedback. To get bulletproof memory:

🚫 Top 6 Mistakes to Avoid

1. Overusing "Easy"

Pressing Easy on problems that feel familiar pushes them months into the future. Reserve Easy strictly for questions you could code in your sleep.

2. Rating "Good" After Looking at Hints

If you had to look at your notes to remember the data structure, you did not recall it. Press Again!

3. Pressing "Again" Over Syntax Typos

The AlgoRecall Engine is testing algorithmic intuition, not language syntax memorization. If the logic was correct, press Hard.

4. Letting Overdue Cards Pile Up

If you let 50 reviews pile up, your retrievability drops across all cards. Review 5–10 cards daily instead of 50 once a month.

5. Cramming Outside the Schedule

Reviewing cards early yields minimal stability gains because memory decay hasn't occurred yet.

6. Unrealistic Target Retention (e.g. 98%)

Setting target retention to 97%+ in settings exponentially multiplies your daily review workload for marginal recall gain.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Q: Why did my card come back for review just 1–5 minutes after I created it?

When you logged the card in the floating widget, you pressed 🔴 Again or 🟡 Hard. In the AlgoRecall Engine, this immediately initiates Stage 1 (Learning practice mode). Learning cards use short-interval steps (~1–5 minutes) to ensure initial memory encoding before graduating to multi-day intervals.

Q: Why is my newly created card not listed under the "New" stage in my dashboard?

In traditional flashcard apps, you type cards into a database days before studying them. In AlgoRecall, you log cards in-page right after attempting a problem on LeetCode or AlgoMonster. Because you just solved or attempted the problem, the floating bar prompt reads:

Save & Rate Initial Difficulty: [Again] [Hard] [Good] [Easy]

When you click one of these buttons, the extension creates the card (internally State.New) and immediately applies your selected button as your first review!

  • If you press 🔴 Again or 🟡 Hard: The card immediately moves from New → 📖 Learning (short practice check-in in ~1–10 minutes).
  • If you press 🟢 Good or 🔵 Easy: The card immediately graduates from New → 🏆 Review (long-term memory, due in 1–4+ days).

Result: Cards do not sit idle in the unstudied "New" stage; the AlgoRecall Engine immediately schedules your next review date from your very first click!

Q: What does "Graduated" or "Mastered" mean in AlgoRecall?

A card is considered Graduated when it has completed practice/relearning and achieved a memory stability of greater than 7 days (stability > 7). In analytics and badges, these cards count as established long-term knowledge.

Q: What happens if I take a vacation and miss reviewing for a week?

Don't panic! The AlgoRecall Engine does not delete your cards or wipe out your progress. Due cards simply accumulate in your review queue sorted by due date. When you return, review them at your own pace. If you still remember an overdue card, the AlgoRecall Engine will actually reward it with a higher stability gain!

Q: If I answer "Again", does the AlgoRecall Engine reset my card back to zero?

No! Unlike older SM-2 systems that wiped out all historical progress, the AlgoRecall Engine remembers that you previously mastered the problem. It temporarily drops stability and places the card into Relearning, but once you complete a quick refresher check, stability recovers much faster than starting from scratch.

Q: Why do two cards reviewed on the same day have different next review dates?

The AlgoRecall Engine calculates dates independently for every card based on its historical repetition count, inherent difficulty rating, topic weights, and past lapses. A simple Hash Map problem will naturally have higher stability than an advanced Dynamic Programming problem.

Q: What happens if I accidentally click the wrong rating button?

AlgoRecall features built-in Algorithmic Debouncing & Misclick Correction. If you click the intended rating button within 60 seconds, the extension automatically rolls back to the card's previous snapshot and recalculates cleanly with your corrected rating.

Q: Should I review cards before they are due?

Generally, no. Spaced repetition works because memory consolidation occurs right as memory traces begin to decay. Reviewing cards when retrievability is near 100% produces negligible stability increases and adds unnecessary workload.

Q: Can I remove or delete a card I no longer want to practice?

Yes. You can click the Remove button directly inside the floating in-page tracker, or use the Data Dashboard (Advanced Tab) to search, filter, and delete cards individually or in bulk.

Q: Does the AlgoRecall Engine guarantee I will never forget a problem?

Spaced repetition is probabilistic, not absolute. With the default 88% retention setting, you are mathematically expected to remember ~88 out of 100 problems when they become due. The other 12% trigger lapses (refresher rounds), which identify your weak spots and reinforce them.

Q: How does the floating review queue determine which card to show next?

The review queue filters for all cards where due ≤ now and sorts them ascending by due date (a.due - b.due). This ensures the most overdue cards are always shown first. You can also filter the session by specific topic tags (e.g. Graphs, Trees).

Q: How can I customize my target retention or algorithm weights?

Open the extension popup, click the settings gear, and open AlgoRecall Engine Settings. You can adjust your target retention rate (0.70 to 0.98), tune custom weights per topic tag, or train optimized parameters once you have logged 50+ reviews.

🧠 Understanding AlgoRecall Engine Parameters (w0 – w16)

The AlgoRecall Engine uses 17 coefficients to compute memory stability updates upon grading:

  • Target Retention Slider (0.75 - 0.95): Sets desired recall probability. Higher retention (95%) yields shorter intervals; lower retention (80%) spreads reviews further apart.
  • Initial Stability (w0 – w3): Base interval days for Again, Hard, Good, Easy on first review.
  • Difficulty & Stability Boosts (w4 – w10): Rate of stability increase for correct answers.
  • Lapses & Easy Bonuses (w11 – w16): Recovery deceleration upon failure and easy bonuses.

🏷️ Tag-Specific Profiles
